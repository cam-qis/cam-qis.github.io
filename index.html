About me

I am a PhD student working at the intersection of atomic, molecular, and optical (AMO) physics and quantum engineering, with a focus on solid-state quantum systems. My research follows two complementary directions: scaling quantum information systems and using precision quantum measurements to probe fundamental physics.

My work spans solid-state spin–photon interfaces, integrated quantum photonics, coherent control, and fault-tolerant quantum architectures. I am interested in both the physics of individual quantum systems and the experimental and systems-level engineering needed to use them.

Research

Scaling quantum information systems

Building toward larger quantum systems by connecting solid-state spin qubits, optical interfaces, and fault-tolerant architectures. My previous work includes silicon-vacancy (SiV) centers at MITRE, T centers in silicon at UC Berkeley, and modular quantum computing architectures at EPFL.

Probing fundamental physics with quantum systems

Using coherent control and precision spectroscopy in solid-state AMO platforms to search for subtle signatures of new physics. My current focus is europium-doped yttrium orthosilicate (Eu:YSO) and measurements sensitive to fundamental symmetry violations.
